NewsMuncher Phase 4C — large-scale comedy vocabulary expansion
Date: 2026-10-10

Scope and safety
================

Phase 4C expands the offline typed catalogue established in Phase 4B. It does
not alter live MUNGE selectors, prompts, leases, cooldowns, claims, MongoDB,
Gallery or media generation. The audit made zero database or provider calls.
One bounded development-time download used the official Open English WordNet
2025 JSON release; the archive was extracted under /tmp and is not stored in
the repository.

Why Phase 4B was small
======================

Phase 4B contained 134 animals and 508 objects because it admitted the 60 and
200 previously reviewed generator bases, 74 explicitly labelled animal review
candidates and only 308 legacy entries that a conservative physical-object
filter could identify. It did not pretend that all 2,119 mixed values in
animalsAndObjects.csv were distinct usable identities.

Its 708 descriptors were the structured generator components whose insertion
roles were known. Phase 4A's 2,389 figure included flat adverbs.csv candidates
that mix adjectives, participles, adverbs, spelling variants and ordinary
qualities without grammatical or provenance metadata. The 30 slang entries
were a small safe sample because the legacy slang file contains substantial
graphic and discriminatory material. The 58 place entries were original
fictional morphology samples; places.csv mixes real geography, businesses,
landmarks, fiction and explicit invented locations and cannot safely be called
a fictional-component bank.

The exclusions were therefore primarily editorial, grammatical, licensing and
semantic, not architectural. Phase 4C recovers suitable physical-object and
descriptor material through typed imports, but deliberately does not bulk-admit
the unsafe slang or real-place CSVs.

Architecture and sources
========================

The schema remains version 1.0; the catalogue becomes phase4c-1. The existing
stable ID, grammar, semantic class, compatible mechanisms, familiarity,
provenance, licence, flags and approval fields remain. Category-specific fields
now include OEWN synset IDs and definitions, object affordances, slang register,
meaning and safety state, and fictional-place construction markers.

Open English WordNet 2025 Edition
---------------------------------

The official 2025 JSON release supplied taxonomic/common animal labels,
artifact nouns, adjectives and adverbs. Open English WordNet is CC BY 4.0 and
derived from Princeton WordNet; both attribution and upstream synset identity
are retained. The reproducible importer records the source URL, release,
filters and attribution. Familiarity ranking uses wordfreq 3.1.1 (Apache 2.0),
recorded separately from entry provenance and required only by the importer.
It rejects malformed labels, obvious anatomical and
taxonomic groupings, a conservative unsafe-term set and very low-recognition
entries, then ranks candidates locally. All 7,046 imported entries remain
machine_checked and needs_review.

Local sources
-------------

The 1,438 previously reviewed NewsMuncher entries retain their earlier status.
There are 308 conservatively filtered legacy objects, 186 Phase 4A samples and
1,002 original Phase 4C components. Original additions include safe labelled
colloquialisms, surname-root candidates, fictional morphology and institutions.
They are machine_checked, never represented as human-reviewed, and retain
INTERNAL-UNVERIFIED provenance.

Wikidata's structured data was confirmed as CC0, but a bounded given-name query
did not return usable results during this phase, so no Wikidata data was
imported. GeoNames was confirmed CC BY 4.0 but was not imported: its real places
would require a separate morphology derivation and factual-location guard.
GBIF and Wiktionary remain excluded for the licence reasons recorded in Phase
4A. No source provenance has been invented.

to manufacturing weak aliases or importing unsafe legacy phrases.

Animals now carry twelve selection classes including mammals, birds, reptiles,

and medical equipment, musical instruments, machinery, containers, furniture

temporal and degree classes. Names add object-derived surname roots; slang adds
exclamations, insults, idioms, intensifiers and reactions; places add lexical
stems, place types and fictional institutions.

Validation and editorial status
================================

Automated validation reports zero errors, two near-duplicate warnings and five
semantic-concentration warnings. The warnings are retained because OEWN's
lexicographer files are broader than the comedy-specific classification:
978 animals remain other_animal, 2,425 objects physical_object, 1,809
descriptors general_description, 421 names object_surname_root and 476 places
fictional_morphology. The two near variants are out-of-door/out-of-doors and
finger/fingers.

The validator covers duplicate IDs/text, inflectional near variants, malformed
phrases, descriptor roles, missing provenance, unsupported licences,
discriminatory content, trademark/real-person risk flags, false editorial
approval, unsupported obscurity and concentration. Near-duplicate lookup is
indexed rather than quadratic, keeping the ten-thousand-entry validation fast.

Machine validation cannot determine whether an ambiguous label denotes a
recognisable animal/object in isolation or whether a lexical descriptor is a
good comic situation. New entries must pass sampled human review before shadow
or live eligibility.

250-MUNGE diversity audit
=========================

Both catalogues used normal suggestion quotas: four animals, sixteen objects,
and ten each of descriptors, name ingredients, slang and place ingredients.
The audit separately modelled randomly accepted/used subsets, because offered
ingredients are not necessarily used by a rewrite. It does not claim that
offline diversity guarantees diverse model output.

Family       Unique offered B→C   Unique used B→C   rolling-10 B→C   C semantic classes
Animals             133→518             115→261          0→0              12
Objects             506→1,462           325→563          0→0              19
Descriptors         699→695             484→453          0→0              36
Names               478→522             298→301          0→0              17
Slang                30→128              30→79       68.51→0               5
Places               58→467              47→99       44.09→0               3

Animal and object identity diversity improves materially. Safe slang and place
expansion removes short-window repetition, although lifetime reuse remains high
because those banks are still small. Name-root improvement is modest.
Descriptor diversity is flat despite the larger bank: generic OEWN adjectives
mostly occupy semantic classes already covered by stronger reviewed comedy
ingredients, so semantic balancing correctly continues to favour distinct
reviewed situations. This demonstrates that lexical volume cannot replace new
comic mechanisms, subjects and circumstances.

The reconstructed Phase 4B audit took 6,494.43 ms; Phase 4C took 43,263.71 ms.
Peak traced memory was 36,950,691 bytes. The current selector repeatedly sorts
large pools and is suitable for offline review, not yet for live integration.
A future shadow implementation should pre-index family/role/mechanism buckets
and cache seeded ranks. Database and provider operations were zero.

Weak and rejected examples
==========================

Weak accepted-for-review labels expose the limits of automated lexical import:
`small beer` is an abstract OEWN artifact sense; `mechanical drawing` is visual
rather than a manipulable object; `nanny` and `kitty` are ambiguous animal
labels; generic adjectives such as `exclusive` and `patient` add little comic
premise; object-derived name roots such as `Toilet`, `Automatic` and `Setup`
need strong construction rules; fictional stems such as `Garage` and `Cooling`
do not automatically sound like places.

Rejected examples include `gastric mill`, `green gland`, `animal leg`, `bill`,
`claw`, `quill`, `fetus` and `goose down` because they are anatomy, life stages
or animal products rather than animal identities. Graphic or discriminatory
legacy slang such as `dildo`, `shemale`, `spastic`, `camel jockey`, `towel head`
and `christ killer` is excluded rather than merely down-weighted. Real names
from names.csv and real locations from places.csv were not repackaged as
invented identities.

Remaining gaps and controlled integration
=========================================

1. Conduct stratified human review by animal/object semantic class, marking
   ambiguous member labels rejected and refining taxonomy/affordances.
2. Add genuinely new descriptor situations and mechanisms. Generic adjective
   imports should remain optional lexical support, not replace the approved
   comedy descriptor generator.
3. Expand names with a separately licensed given-name dataset and editorial
   phonetic review. Preserve 70/30 cheeky/absurd construction in the generator.
4. Expand slang only through safe role-labelled review. The rejected legacy
   bank must never be bulk-enabled.
5. Derive fictional morphology from a licensed geography snapshot without
   emitting real names, retaining source-feature IDs and transformation rules.
6. Optimize selector indexing, then shadow one family at a time. Start with
   reviewed animal/object candidates behind configuration; compare exact use,
   grammar and premise repetition before any live switch.
7. Preserve current registries and accepted-use memory. Catalogue IDs should
   become additional lease/cooldown keys only after shadow validation.
