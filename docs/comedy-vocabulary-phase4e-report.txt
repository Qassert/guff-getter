NewsMuncher Phase 4E — descriptors, names, slang and fictional places
Date: 2026-10-11

Scope and safety
================

Phase 4E preserves all Phase 4D animal/object work. It adds independently gated
descriptor and invented-name evaluation layers plus offline-only typed slang
and fictional-place generators. Defaults, prompt slot counts, rewrite UUIDs,
registries, leases, cooldowns, accepted-use matching, historical creations and
provider count remain unchanged. No production database, network or provider
operation was made.

Confirmed repetition causes
===========================

Descriptors: 24 old templates conceal small joke-bearing subject/situation
banks. Slate ranking repeatedly preferred reliable impossibility, medical,
ritual and embarrassment structures. Phrase cooldown is correct; the editorial
mechanism space is the limit.

Names: millions of combinations are dominated by 46 cheeky and 36 absurd roots,
the same suffix rhythms and twelve recipes. Exact-name cooldown and fresh UUID
selection work correctly. Ingredient sound families, not full-name capacity,
are the constraint.

Slang: the live CSV is large but flat, role-incompatible and uneven in safety.
The typed Phase 4C bank has only 128 entries. Neither gives structured control
over insult, exclamation, idiom, comparison or mock-outrage roles.

Places: the live CSV mixes real geography, institutions and invented phrases.
The typed catalogue has components but insufficient morphology. Repetition is
driven by a few suffixes and institutional jokes, while factual-location safety
requires keeping replacement optional and explicit.

New resources and mechanisms
============================

Descriptors add 12 machine-checked mechanisms and templates, bringing the
resource to 36 templates and 32 mapped mechanisms. Added situations cover
peculiar habits, romantic complications, medical oddities, qualifications,
workplace failures, irrational fears, ambitions, scandals, hobbies, rivalries,
personal legal restrictions and unlikely achievements. All six grammatical
roles remain available and the existing 70/30 category plus 30/45/25 intensity
allocation is unchanged. Two compatible ingredient slots give each new
mechanism 100 deterministic constructions rather than adjective substitution.

Names add 32 cheeky given names, 20 classic surnames, 20 roots, 15 suffixes and
8 nicknames. Absurd names add 24 given names, 12 classic surnames, 12 roots, 12
suffixes, 5 neutral titles, 5 epithets and 7 territories. Existing recipes,
filters and 70/30 weighting remain. All additions are original machine-checked
review material, not real identities.

Slang adds five typed mechanisms backed by 60 components: mild insult, comic
disbelief, useless comparison, failed-organisation idiom and mock outrage.
Places add seven construction types backed by 70 components: settlements,
roads, estates, institutions, venues and geographical features. They are
original `INTERNAL-UNVERIFIED` machine-checked material and make no claim of
authentic historic or regional usage.

Controlled integration
======================

  NEWSMUNCHER_EXPANDED_DESCRIPTORS=off|reviewed|evaluation
  NEWSMUNCHER_EXPANDED_NAMES=off|reviewed|evaluation

Both default to `off`. `reviewed` currently behaves like `off` because Phase 4E
adds no human-reviewed entry. `evaluation` merges the expansion into the
existing generator while retaining the existing Mongo registry and prompt ten.
Invalid values fail configuration. Generator failure follows the existing typed
409 and lease cleanup path. Slang and places remain offline because their small
constructed spaces repeat more than the current CSVs and they lack a dedicated
accepted-use registry.

places are clearly below the live baseline and remain offline.

Editorial assessment
====================

Strong descriptor examples include “saluting unattended luggage before anyone
can object”, “engaged to a disputed scarecrow by mutual misunderstanding”,
“terrified of confidently folded maps since the indoor regatta”, and “legally
barred from unsupervised confidence pending a tribunal of concerned aunties”.

Strong name examples include Neville Nudgeham, Lady Tabitha Thistlecrank,
Provisional Chancellor Godfrey Ganderduckworth and Wilhelmina Mouldering-Fox,
Patron of Unnecessary Trumpets. New roots appear naturally, but familiar old
contours still dominate too many slates.

Strong offline expressions include “about as useful as a marzipan crowbar”,
“you championship-grade clipboard peacock”, “couldn't supervise a sandwich in
a panic warehouse”, North Ditherleigh, Apology Lane, and the Bureau of
Unclaimed Spoons.

Weak/rejected material includes overlong pre-noun qualifications, combinations
whose context adds explanation without a stronger punchline, repeated
“constitutionally incapable” structures, generic name suffix swaps, forced
“well, butter …” exclamations and settlement names whose stem/suffix join is
hard to pronounce. Generation rejects exact duplicates, over-14-word slang/place
outputs, blocked name forms and existing name filters. Editorially weak but safe
items remain labelled machine-checked in the sample rather than being hidden.

Licensing and editorial status
==============================

All Phase 4E additions are original repository-local constructions labelled
`machine_checked`; none is silently promoted. Existing catalogue provenance and
Open English WordNet attribution remain unchanged. The live legacy CSV licences
are not reclassified. Real place names and real people are not sources for the
new generators.

Readiness
=========

Descriptors: ready for independent local evaluation only. Mechanism breadth is
promising; ranking, concision and ingredient variety need another editorial pass.

Names: ready for independent local evaluation only. Quality is stable and root
diversity rises modestly, but the improvement is not yet large enough for a new
default.

Slang: offline only. It needs several hundred more reviewed role-compatible
constructions and accepted-use memory before live wiring.

Fictional places: offline only. It needs stronger morphology joining, more
suffix families and explicit factual-place protection in a future registry.

Manual local testing
====================

1. Restart with both new flags unset and confirm unchanged MUNGE output.
2. Set `NEWSMUNCHER_EXPANDED_DESCRIPTORS=evaluation`, restart, and review ten
   MUNGEs for role grammar, repeated mechanisms and prompt uptake.
3. Test names separately with `NEWSMUNCHER_EXPANDED_NAMES=evaluation`.
4. Test both flags together only after separate review. Keep the Phase 4D
   animal/object flag independently configured.
5. Confirm genuine retry stability, fresh MUNGE UUIDs, simultaneous sessions,
   failed-rewrite release and `[munge-timing]` output.
6. Unset either flag and restart for immediate rollback.

Offline commands:

  PYTHONPATH=. .venv/bin/python scripts/diagnostics/build_phase4e_resources.py
  PYTHONPATH=. .venv/bin/python scripts/diagnostics/evaluate_vocabulary_phase4e.py --rewrites 250

Recommended next steps
======================

Tune descriptor slate ranking to rotate mechanisms across accepted history,
shorten the longest two-slot constructions and expand the weakest subjects.
Review name outputs by spoken root family rather than full-name uniqueness.
For slang, expand each grammatical role before building a shared generic phrase
registry. For places, add compatibility tables for stems/suffixes and keep real
source locations exempt. Promote individual resources only after human review;
do not enable all four categories as one switch.

==========

0 failed. Python compilation, all production JavaScript syntax checks, the
reproducible Phase 4E resource build and `git diff --check` passed. The suites
used mocks and local resources; no production database or provider was used.
