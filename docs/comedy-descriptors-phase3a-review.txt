1. EXISTING BANK INVENTORY

adverbs.csv
- 1,954 cells; 1,683 unique after trimming/case-folding; 271 duplicate cells.
- 1,939 single-word entries and 15 multiword entries.
- Despite its filename, this is predominantly an adjective/participle descriptor bank. Examples include angular, angry, abandoned, gleaming, sticky, swollen and threadbare. Some true adverbs/connectives appear, but the live prompt accurately labels the bank "Adverbs/absurd descriptors" and explicitly permits grammatical alteration.
- Strongest styles: tactile/material descriptions, bodily or mechanical movement, visual finish, emotional condition and grotesque physical texture.
- Weaknesses: extensive exact duplication; clusters of near-synonyms around wetness, slime, swelling, pulsing, damage, glow and hair; many ordinary dictionary adjectives; almost no developed comic premise; grammatical role is not recorded.
- Compatibility constraint: a generated replacement cannot be dropped blindly into the existing pre-noun/adverb instruction. Predicate clauses such as "legally required to wear slippers" need explicit role-aware prompt guidance.

slang.csv
- 2,277 cells; 2,142 unique after trimming/case-folding; 135 duplicate cells.
- 1,222 single-word and 1,055 multiword entries.
- It mixes exclamations, insults, anatomical/sexual vocabulary, profanity, compound abuse, sexual acts, descriptive slang and invented vulgar phrases. The filename therefore hides several incompatible grammatical and tonal roles.
- Weaknesses: numerous spelling variants and concatenated forms; repeated stems; a substantial amount of graphic or discriminatory material; many phrases cannot substitute for one another grammatically; familiar profanity dominates subtler British comic language.

places.csv
- 1,005 cells; 997 unique after trimming/case-folding; eight duplicate cells: Dominican Republic, Smingehorpe, Dudley, Iceland, Boots, Stonehenge, Dartmoor and Twat-Piss Peak.
- 397 single-word and 608 multiword entries.
- It mixes real countries, regions, settlements and landmarks with businesses, institutions and invented/rude locations. Those roles carry different prepositions and different risks to source geography.
- Weaknesses: no real/invented distinction, no geographic type, no preposition metadata, and no mechanism for retaining a source story's real geographic context.

2. ACTUAL LIVE CODE PATH

- clean_data.CSV_FILES maps the three prompt keys to these files. In generated-concept mode only nouns and animals are excluded; adverbs, slang and places still use the legacy path.
- load_random_words reads each bank through word_shuffle.read_bank, which strips boundary whitespace and deduplicates while retaining spelling/capitalisation. PermanentWordClaims.draw shuffles the currently unclaimed vocabulary and supplies ten values from each bank.
- prepare_prompt source-filters the candidates, splits each ten-value list into three interleaved placeholder lists and inserts them under Adverbs/absurd descriptors, Places and Slang/rude words.
- The rewrite prompt asks for most adjectives/adverbs and most places to be replaced, while slang is optional where it improves the joke. The generated noun/animal overrides and invented-name override occupy separate prompt fields and do not alter these three banks.
- After the existing two-pass rewrite, claim_used_words performs exact, case-insensitive boundary matching against the final title/body. Only supplied values literally used are atomically committed to word_shuffle_bags.
- Claims are permanent. There is no time cooldown or recycling. Concurrent draws may overlap, but the Mongo transaction rejects a final claim if another rewrite claimed the same candidate first. Unused candidates are never claimed.

3. STANDALONE DESCRIPTOR GENERATOR

Files:
- newsmuncher/services/comedy_descriptors.py
- newsmuncher/resources/descriptors/comedy_descriptor_components.json
- scripts/diagnostics/generate_comedy_descriptors.py
- tests/python/test_comedy_descriptors.py

Architecture:
- Two configurable editorial categories: cheeky_british (70%) and broader_absurdity (30%).
- Three configurable intensity levels: 30% mild, 45% properly absurd and 25% unhinged.
- Six grammatical roles: pre_noun, adverbial, predicate, post_noun, behavioural and short_clause.
- Sixteen construction templates across thirteen comic mechanisms. Templates, rather than uncontrolled adjective stacking, determine which component banks can combine.
- Deterministic seeded selection, exact largest-remainder allocations, normalized duplicate prevention, bounded candidate search, template/subject diversity caps, blocked-term filtering, grammatical rejection rules, quality scores and visible editorial warnings.
- Every output records phrase, grammatical role, mechanism, category, intensity, template, version, word count, score, warnings and a diagnostic subject.
- The module is not imported by previews.py or clean_data.py and cannot affect live Creation rewriting.

Editorial observations:
- Successful concise forms include "professionally overexcited", "chronically underqualified" and "emotionally well-moisturised".
- Strong developed premises include bans for tactical scones, constitutional inability to recognise Tuesdays, medical warnings against advanced tutting, and petty campaigns over badly chaired meetings.
- Grammatical roles prevent emotional, legal, medical and historical clauses from entering a pre-noun slot.
- Remaining borderline material is concentrated in long circumstance clauses and the medical-source construction. These are retained and explicitly visible in the weakest-output list rather than hidden by score manipulation.
- Some monitored themes remain, but the audit's most common subjects are circumstances rather than biscuits, sausages, custard, trousers, councils or ceremonies. No monitored theme is structurally required.

4. FUTURE SLANG GENERATOR PLAN

Recommended categories/roles:
- exclamation: sentence-level reactions with punctuation metadata;
- direct insult: vocative or predicate noun, with target/safety metadata;
- descriptive slang: adjective or predicate description;
- comic oath: standalone interjection;
- cheeky euphemism: noun phrase with explicit tone/intensity;
- action/expression: clause or verb phrase.

Use curated British mechanisms, safety tiers and grammatical roles. Keep explicit sexual material behind a deliberate configuration rather than mixing it with general exclamations. Never infer interchangeability from the current CSV. A future integration should offer optional role-labelled phrases and track accepted uses in a separate lease/cooldown ledger, leaving the historical CSV and claims intact.

5. FUTURE PLACE GENERATOR PLAN

Recommended categories/metadata:
- real source place (preserved, never randomly replaced without editorial intent);
- real British place with type and correct in/at/from prepositions;
- plausible invented settlement;
- ridiculous civic facility;
- landscape/landmark;
- institution/business presented as a location;
- ceremonial or bureaucratic jurisdiction.

Each item needs place type, real/invented status, country/region where applicable, suitable prepositions and whether it may replace a source location. The safest future integration keeps at least one recognisable source geography and treats comic places as optional secondary locations. Blind replacement of every place risks changing the central meaning and producing misinformation-like geography.
