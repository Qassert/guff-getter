NewsMuncher Phase 3E — cross-generator repetition and diversity review
Date: 2026-10-09

Scope and safety
================

This investigation used deterministic local generators, in-memory Mongo-shaped
collections and mocked rewrite outcomes. It made no provider request and no
production database write. The live prompt still contains 20 concepts and 10
descriptors; the two-pass rewrite flow, legacy modes, rewrite identity, saved
creations and media paths are unchanged.

Confirmed causes
================

1. Phrase uniqueness was masking structural repetition. Before this change the
   concept generator had 17 templates and 15 mechanisms, while the descriptor
   generator had 16 templates and 13 mechanisms. Large object/subject banks
   produced many unique strings, but the central joke-bearing modifier banks
   were often only 6–10 entries per intensity. Quality ranking repeatedly
   preferred the same productive structures.

2. Cooldown keys represented only part of the repeated joke. Concept selection
   protected base objects, and descriptor selection protected subjects, but
   neither represented the broader semantic premise. Thus differently worded
   council, restriction, secret-history or impossible-ability jokes could be
   considered fresh.

3. Descriptor selection cooled an entire preassembled slate. It discarded a
   slate when any subject was unavailable instead of assembling a valid slate
   from the wider candidate pool. In the production-shaped pre-change audit,
   ordinary accepted-use saturation caused bounded exhaustion after only 4–6
   consecutive rewrites. Concepts had already needed a related saturation
   workaround during Phase 2E.

4. Unused offers were correctly released and failed rewrites did not cool them.
   Exact accepted-use normalization behaved conservatively as designed. This
   means exact phrases used verbatim are tracked, while paraphrases cannot be
   reliably inferred without another model call. That limitation is distinct
   from a lease leak.

5. Deterministic seeds were not themselves repeating a slate: each new rewrite
   UUID creates a new deterministic pool and a genuine retry retains its slate.
   The restricted mechanisms and cooldown representation were the material
   causes.

6. Concepts and descriptors were assembled independently. This allowed one
   prompt to contain several unrelated jokes from the same broad theme.

Implementation
==============

Structured diversity metadata
-----------------------------

Every generated concept and descriptor now records a stable semantic_theme and
premise_key. Premise keys combine a deliberately bounded semantic theme with
the joke-bearing modifier or subject. They are deterministic generator
metadata rather than fuzzy similarity scores.

Concept registry
----------------

Concept selection now atomically leases the exact phrase, base object and
semantic premise. Accepted exact uses cool all three records; unused offers and
failed rewrites release all three. A slate forbids duplicate bases and premises.
Phrase cooldown always remains strict. If finite base/premise cooldowns make an
otherwise valid 20-item slate impossible, only those diversity cooldowns relax;
exact phrase cooldown, active leases, quota rules and concurrency safety remain
hard. This prevents repeated-MUNGE exhaustion without silently reverting to CSV
words.

Descriptor registry
-------------------

Descriptor selection now creates one broad deterministic candidate pool,
filters known phrase/subject availability with a single read, then assembles a
grammar-complete 10-item slate from eligible candidates. Only the final slate
is leased atomically. Exact phrase cooldown remains strict. Subject cooldown
can relax on demonstrated saturation while active leases, exact phrases,
category/intensity quotas and all six grammatical roles remain hard.

Cross-generator balancing
-------------------------

The rewrite path counts concept semantic themes. A theme is passed to descriptor
assembly only when it occurs at least three times in the concept slate.
Descriptor ranking softly deprioritizes those themes while preserving all hard
grammar/category/intensity constraints. No metadata is added to the provider
prompt, so this adds no prompt tokens or provider calls.

New mechanisms
--------------

The concept resource now has 24 templates, adding genuinely different comic
situations: domestic alibis, scandalous reputations, impossible measurement,
principled creature refusals, absurd dispute resolution, emergency aftermath
and commemorated failure.

The descriptor resource now has 24 templates, adding meteorological affliction,
domestic supernaturalism, financial folly, temporal quirks, mistaken
relationships, invented ritual status, compulsory ritual and cosmic
motivation. Existing British irreverence, innuendo, institutional absurdity,
short forms and elaborate clauses remain.

Offline simulation
==================

Method
------

The command is:

  PYTHONPATH=. .venv/bin/python scripts/diagnostics/evaluate_comedy_diversity.py --seed phase3e-final --rewrites 100

Each rewrite uses the production 20-concept and 10-descriptor quotas. It marks
2–4 randomly selected candidates as exact accepted uses, one candidate as a
local paraphrase that conservative matching intentionally does not recognize,
and the remainder as ignored. Each selection has a separate rewrite identity;
registry tests separately cover simultaneous sessions and atomic collision
handling.

The pre-change production-shaped runs could not reach their requested 10, 25,
50 or 100 checkpoints: depending on the deterministic seed, descriptor
selection exhausted after 4–6 rewrites. This was a real availability defect,
not evidence of phrase exhaustion.

Rolling ten-rewrite exact phrase repetition
-------------------------------------------

At 100 rewrites, the concept rolling-window average was 2.63 repeated offers
out of 200 (maximum 7). The descriptor average was 7.11 out of 100 (maximum
15). Premise repetition is necessarily higher: the audit recorded averages of
70.10/200 concepts and 31.84/100 descriptors because semantic themes and their
joke-bearing component banks are intentionally bounded and editorially curated.

Accepted versus offered
-----------------------

Across 100 rewrites the simulation accepted 296 concept phrases and 303
descriptor phrases exactly. It modelled 100 paraphrased candidates in each
generator as deliberately untracked, and ignored 1,604 concept plus 597
descriptor offers. Diversity cooldown relaxation occurred on 55 concept
selections and 18 descriptor selections. All 100 rewrites completed.

Compared with the intermediate selection-only implementation, resource
expansion reduced 100-rewrite exact repeated occurrences from 186 to 172 for
concepts and from 227 to 204 for descriptors. More importantly, the visible
mechanism coverage rose from 15 to 19 concepts and from roughly 12 to 20
descriptors, and the descriptor registry stopped exhausting after a few
rewrites.

Examples of earlier recurring structures
-----------------------------------------

The old audit repeatedly favoured powered appliances, compact misbehaving
animals, secret histories, medical prohibitions and social-ritual clauses.
Lexically different examples could therefore amount to another powered device,
another creature under official sanction, or another person banned from an
activity. Those phrases were not exact duplicates but shared the same comic
engine.

Trade-offs and remaining limitations
====================================

* Premise metadata is explicit and explainable, but coarse. It cannot recognize
  every paraphrase or two unrelated constructions that a reader considers the
  same joke. Adding embedding or model similarity would add cost, latency and
  false-positive risk and was deliberately rejected.
* Cooldowns apply only to exact candidates found in the accepted title/body.
  This protects against false claims but cannot cool a model paraphrase.
* Twenty concepts per rewrite produce 2,000 offers in 100 rewrites, so reuse of
  a finite set of visually useful bases is expected. Unused offers remain
  eligible by design; otherwise prompt candidates would exhaust rapidly.
* Saturation fallback trades some base/subject/premise freshness for reliable
  completion. It never relaxes exact phrase cooldown or active lease ownership.
* Registries now create/read additional phrase, base/subject and premise
  records. A normal concept slate can perform up to 60 atomic lease writes and
  a descriptor slate up to 20, followed by existing accepted/release updates.
  The broad descriptor availability read avoids repeatedly attempting known
  unavailable candidates. This is local database work and adds no provider
  cost or prompt tokens.
* Cross-generator balancing is intentionally soft. A hard ban could make role
  or category quotas impossible and reduce humour quality.

Manual review
=============

1. Restart NewsMuncher so the new resource versions are loaded.
2. Create at least ten MUNGEs, including nominate-then-MUNGE-again from the same
   source. Check that no ordinary sequence produces a 409 or stuck button.
3. Review each candidate article for exact repeated phrases and for repeated
   premises such as councils, medical restrictions, secret occupations and
   social rituals.
4. Confirm a genuine retry of one in-progress rewrite preserves its offered
   slate, while a new rewrite UUID produces a fresh slate.
5. Run two browser sessions concurrently and confirm both complete without
   receiving overlapping actively leased phrases/premises.
6. If a controlled 409 occurs under artificial saturation, confirm the Creator
   displays the actionable message and restores MUNGE availability.
7. Legacy modes remain available through the existing name, concept and
   descriptor configuration switches.

Manual-test correction
======================

The first live manual attempt exposed a timezone representation mismatch in
the descriptor availability prefilter. PyMongo decodes BSON datetimes as naive
UTC by default, while the registry clock is aware UTC. Comparing a persisted
`last_used_at` directly with the aware cooldown cutoff raised `TypeError`
before any rewrite provider call. Persisted `last_used_at` and `offer_until`
values are now normalized to aware UTC for client-side availability checks.
Database-side atomic lease/cooldown queries and all expiry durations are
unchanged. A regression test covers naive Mongo-shaped timestamps.
