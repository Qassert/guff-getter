SCOPE
- Standalone descriptor generator only. It is not imported by the live rewrite path.
- Six grammatical roles, sixteen templates, thirteen comic mechanisms, 70/30 category mix and 30/45/25 intensity mix are retained.
- No CSV, claim, concept, name, provider, creation, Gallery or media behaviour changed.

EDITORIAL CHANGES
- Replaced the weak generic competence construction (for example, “chronically underqualified”) with concise attributive comic contradictions such as “emotionally raffle-certified” and “theatrically committee-proof”.
- Removed ordinary grievance subjects such as a badly chaired meeting. Petty reactions now target premises that carry their own comic surprise.
- Added an explicit basis to inappropriate-confidence descriptions, so “certain they could inspect a nuclear submarine” becomes forms such as “certain they could inspect a nuclear submarine with one teaspoon”.
- Made institutional, legal, medical, motive and capability second clauses explain or intensify the first premise rather than attach unrelated filler.
- Retained short two- and three-word forms. Long forms receive no automatic score advantage; phrases over ten words are flagged for editorial review, and the 16-word safety ceiling is used only for structured premises whose second clause supplies the payoff.

GRAMMATICAL CONTRACT
- pre_noun: place directly before a person or character noun. Example: “The emotionally raffle-certified mayor opened the meeting.”
- predicate: requires a linking verb such as is/was. Example: “The mayor is medically advised against advanced tutting.”
- post_noun: comma-delimited postmodifier after a noun. Example: “The mayor, banned from competitive queueing for concealing a tactical scone, opened the meeting.”
- adverbial: follows an action and supplies motive/circumstance. Example: “The mayor opened the meeting to avoid small talk against medical advice.”
- behavioural: comma-delimited ongoing behaviour after a noun. Example: “The mayor, operating under an assumed bus timetable to evade the village ombudsman, opened the meeting.”
- short_clause: a complete clause when the template supplies its subject, otherwise a partial clause following a named subject. Every emitted item now includes both an insertion rule and deterministic usage example.

EFFICIENCY AND ACCOUNTING
Phase 3A’s “proposed” count included accepted pool candidates which were never emitted, but its report labelled only explicit failures as rejected. Phase 3B records four disjoint quantities:
- proposed: every constructed candidate inspected;
- rejected: candidates failing grammar, safety, uniqueness or diversity gates;
- eligible: candidates admitted to a local ranking pool;
- emitted versus eligible-not-selected: the winner and valid alternatives in those pools.
Therefore proposed = rejected + eligible, and eligible = emitted + eligible-not-selected.

- Proposal volume fell 67.0%; explicit rejection volume fell 73.1%. This comes from smaller ranking pools, premise-compatible component banks and better subject tracking, not weakened uniqueness or diversity requirements.
- Rejections: duplicate 41,771; repeated word 17,332; template diversity cap 908; word count 455; quality score 15. No blocked, awkward-join, role or template mismatch appeared in this seed.

EDITORIAL ASSESSMENT
- The weakest reviewed outputs still contain an identifiable premise (institutional sponsorship, petty retaliation, an absurd motive, implausible confidence, or physical impossibility). They are no longer merely ordinary adjectives with novelty adverbs.
- The weakest remaining tendency is length: some institutional and motive forms are grammatically clean but carry two beats. These are explicitly marked “long descriptor” for later editorial tuning.
- Score is a bounded construction heuristic, not a claim that a phrase is funny. The weakest/strongest lists are role-balanced (five from each role), preventing one compact high-scoring template from dominating the review.
- Repeated-subject diagnostics now track the semantic joke core rather than the final circumstance slot. The leading subjects are medical actions and petty topics at roughly 39–44 uses each (under 0.9% of the audit); none of the monitored custard/sausage/biscuit/trouser/council/royal themes dominates.
- Compared with Phase 3A, generation is faster and clearer, concise contradictions remain common, confidence phrases have a comic basis, and institutional clauses have causal connections. Variety remains sufficient for 5,000 unique outputs without exhaustion.
