NewsMuncher Phase 4F — comedy diversity optimisation
Date: 2026-10-11

Scope and safety
================

This phase compares the live defaults, the exact Phase 4E experiments and the
Phase 4F changes with identical seeds, quotas and mocked uptake over 250 MUNGEs.
All work is local. There were no provider, network or database operations. The
animal/object integration, historical creations, rewrite identity, leases,
cooldowns, accepted-use memory, HTTP 409 handling and timing instrumentation are
unchanged.

Confirmed Phase 4E causes
=========================

The Phase 4E audit accidentally included the configuration label in its seed.
Current and expanded results therefore used different random streams. The new
audit uses `phase4f:<number>` for all three configurations. The corrected result
still confirms the material weaknesses, but removes seed noise.

Descriptors were limited by selection, not by theoretical expansion capacity.
Assembly sorted every compatible candidate by the same quality hierarchy. The
new mechanisms tended to be longer, while compact old phrases repeatedly won
the quality and word-count tie-breaks. Only 17 of 36 available templates appeared
in Phase 4E and subject/premise diversity fell to 146.

Names already have large exact capacity. Recipe weights and highly productive
constructed surnames dominate output, while the expansion adds comparatively
few new sound shapes. An attempted per-slate recipe equaliser reduced exact and
phonetic diversity, so it was removed. Equivalent-seed evidence shows the Phase
4E expansion modestly improves exact names and roots, but sound patterns fall
from 28 to 27.

Phase 4E slang had five templates. Three were small fixed phrase lists and the
two combinatorial templates had only 144 and 60 possibilities. Its 219 observed
phrases reflect real construction-space scarcity rather than candidate
exhaustion.

Phase 4E places had seven equally selected templates. Several contained only
six fixed phrases, while the larger settlement construction received the same
selection probability. A few stems, suffixes and `View Estate` constructions
therefore dominated short windows.

Improvements
============

Descriptor evaluation mode now randomises within the already filtered,
grammar-compatible, quota-compatible quality stratum. Quality thresholds,
category/intensity quotas, six-role coverage, unique subjects and all registry
protections remain hard. The old Phase 4E ranking remains available internally
to the diagnostic as `phase4e`; the public `evaluation` flag exercises Phase 4F.

Names retain the Phase 4E expansion unchanged. The failed recipe-equalisation
experiment is not present in the final implementation. This is an explicit
decision to preserve better measured behaviour rather than claim an improvement.

Offline slang adds social comparisons, situational absurdity, sarcastic
observations, euphemisms, compound put-downs and additional incompetence and
disbelief structures. Offline places add businesses, landmarks, industrial
parks, composed roads and districts. Selection weights construction families by
bounded phrase capacity so tiny fixed lists cannot dominate. These remain
offline and are not feature flags.

Editorial quality
=================

Strong descriptor premises include “engaged to a disputed scarecrow by mutual
misunderstanding”, “frightened by authoritative knitwear since the disputed
raffle” and “interviewing houseplants under light supervision”. Some repeated
old winners remain, including variants of “banned from … for wearing
performance-enhancing brogues” and “award-winning emergency apologiser”. The
real registry cools accepted exact phrases and subjects; the raw audit purposely
does not hide offered repetition.

Strong names remain pronounceable and structurally varied, but new components
do not create a broader measured consonant/vowel signature set. Names therefore
remain evaluation-only despite improved exact/root metrics.

Strong slang includes “that could sour the mood in an empty lift”, “an
unscheduled dignity leak” and “equipped with the discretion of a village
tannoy”. Weak material includes repeated `what in the …` forms and long compound
put-downs whose second comparison adds bulk more often than a punchline.

Strong places include “Viewing Platform of Premature Optimism”, “The Crooked
Peacock Emporium” and “Indoor Weather Industrial Park”. Weak material includes
ordinary stem/suffix joins such as `Ditherwick`, repeated `View Estate` forms
and businesses whose adjective adds little. Place lifetime diversity now beats
live, but rolling-ten repetition does not.

Acceptance decision
===================

Descriptors are ready for independent local live testing through
`NEWSMUNCHER_EXPANDED_DESCRIPTORS=evaluation`. They improve every measured
descriptor diversity dimension, have no exhaustion, preserve grammar and add
no prompt slots or provider call. They remain machine-checked and disabled by
default.

Names remain available through `NEWSMUNCHER_EXPANDED_NAMES=evaluation`, disabled
by default. They improve exact/root reuse but not phonetic breadth, so Phase 4F
does not recommend promotion.

Slang remains on the existing CSV. The experiment improves greatly over Phase
4E but is still inferior to live exact and rolling diversity. Fictional places
remain offline: lifetime uniqueness beats live, but short-window repetition and
editorial consistency do not. Neither experiment replaces essential factual
story content.

Quality labels and licensing
============================

All additions remain original `machine_checked` repository material under the
existing `INTERNAL-UNVERIFIED` provenance. No output is described as human
reviewed. Existing Open English WordNet attribution and all legacy CSV status
remain unchanged. Editorial criteria are: a clear comic premise, natural spoken
grammar, useful insertion role, recognisable British character, pronounceable
names, no protected-class insult or identity collision, and no modifier that
adds words without strengthening the joke.

Recommended next steps
======================

Manually review at least ten descriptor-evaluation MUNGEs and promote individual
mechanisms only after article-level evidence. Expand name sound families rather
than recipes. For slang, curate more standalone mechanisms before reconsidering
live integration. For places, introduce recent construction-family memory and
morphology compatibility, then require both lifetime and rolling-window metrics
to beat live. Do not activate slang or places from animal/object, descriptor or
name flags.

Manual-test correction: rewrite credit exhaustion
=================================================

A subsequent live attempt proved that local name, concept and descriptor
selection completed, then OpenAI rejected the first rewrite request with HTTP
429, `insufficient_quota` / `credit_balance_exhausted`. `send_prompt()` had
collapsed every provider exception to `None`, so the route reported a generic
500. Credit exhaustion is now a typed provider failure returned as an
actionable HTTP 503. The existing Creator error path displays the message,
stops the loader and restores MUNGE controls; all temporary offers are released
and no automatic paid retry occurs. A successful rewrite still requires API
credits. The live descriptor registry also now applies Phase 4F diversified
assembly in evaluation mode. Final validation is Python 427 passed plus 105
subtests and Node 84 passed.
